Section 01

Overview

Accountability Scrum 2020

The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide, and for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. The SM is a true leader who serves the Scrum Team and the wider organization — one of the three accountabilities (SM, PO, Developers).

The SM serves three ways: the Scrum Team (coaching, facilitating, removing impediments), the Product Owner (backlog & goal techniques), and the Organization (adoption, removing barriers between stakeholders & teams).

SM vs PM vs Agile Coach vs Team Lead

ℹ️
SM is not a mini-PM. See the PM Handbook for project governance and the PO Handbook for product decisions.

Section 03

Servant Leadership

The SM leads by serving — creating the conditions for the team to succeed, not commanding it. Robert Greenleaf

  • Grow people & the team's self-management; make yourself progressively less needed.
  • Protect focus (shield from interruptions), foster psychological safety.
  • Lead change by influence & example, not authority.
  • Hold the team to Scrum values & agreements without dictating how they work.
🔁

Daily ScrumCoach Developers to run it themselves in 15 min.Run it or take status.
Sprint ReviewFacilitate collaboration with stakeholders; keep it a working session.Present the product (that's the team).
RetrospectiveFacilitate honest inspection & concrete improvements.Impose actions or skip it.

ℹ️
SM facilitation is a service, not a requirement — a mature team may need less. The goal is a self-managing team.

Section 05

Impediment Management

An impediment is anything slowing the team that they can't remove themselves. The SM causes their removal.

  1. SURFACEdaily, retro
  2. LOGvisible board
  3. CLASSIFYteam-solvable? org-level?
  4. ACT/ESCALATEremove or escalate to mgmt/PM
  5. VERIFY REMOVEDwith the team

Team-level → coach the team to solve. Organizational → escalate (to PM/management) with impact framed clearly. Track impediments visibly to closure.

Section 07

Facilitation Toolkit

  • Working agreements & a clear purpose for every session.
  • Divergent → convergent flow; timeboxing; parking lot for off-topic items.
  • Techniques: dot voting, fist-of-five, silent brainstorming, round-robin, Liberating Structures.
  • Draw out quiet voices; manage dominators; stay content-neutral as facilitator.
  • Decision methods: consent, consensus, or advice — chosen explicitly.
👥
Section 08

Team Formation & Health

Tuckman stages Bruce Tuckman: Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → (Adjourning). The SM adapts support to the stage — more structure early, more autonomy later.

  • Psychological safety Amy Edmondson is the #1 predictor of team performance (Google Project Aristotle).
  • Foster stable, cross-functional, right-sized teams (Scrum: typically ≤10 people).
  • Watch team-health signals (mood, conflict, dependency, churn); act early.

Section 11

Retrospective Techniques

Vary the format to keep retros fresh & honest Derby & Larsen:

  • Start / Stop / Continue — simple, action-focused.
  • 4 Ls — Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for.
  • Mad / Sad / Glad — surface emotion.
  • Sailboat — wind (helps) vs anchors (blockers).
  • 5-stage: Set the stage → Gather data → Generate insights → Decide actions → Close.
✅
Every retro ends with 1–3 concrete, owned action items — and the next retro reviews them. No actions = theatre.
